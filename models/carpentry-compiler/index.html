Carpentry compiler

Illustrative model based on Wu, Zhao, Nandi, Lipton, Tatlock & Schulz, Carpentry Compiler (ACM TOG 38(6), SIGGRAPH Asia 2019), Sections 4–5. A parametric design (HL-HELM) is compiled into many equivalent low-level cut plans (LL-HELM), which are scored on material cost, fabrication time and precision.

How this model works

      
The design is a sequence of Make_Stock/Make_Cut features (Fig. 4, left). The compiler maps each Make_Cut to Setup_Chopsaw+Chopsaw or to Bandsaw (Section 4), packs parts on stock with the kerf between them (Section 5.2, Fig. 7 uses the same 96″/24″ 2×4 library), and keeps only full cuts. Each plan gets the paper's three objectives (Section 5.3): cost fc = sum of stock prices; precision fp = mean over cuts of (distance of the length to the nearest 1/16″ tape mark) × tool precision weight (chopsaw 1, bandsaw 2); time ft = setups + cuts after setup elimination and stacking (Fig. 8): cuts with the same chopsaw setup share one setup, and equal cuts on independent boards are stacked two-high (a 2×4 stack is 3″, under the chopsaw's 4″ limit quoted in Section 5.2).

      
Simplified: the paper builds an e-graph and extracts plans with NSGA-II; this small design is enumerated directly (random part orders × stock policies × tool choices) and the Pareto front is computed exactly. Time constants (chopsaw setup 60 s, chopsaw cut 15 s, bandsaw cut 40 s, fetch board 10 s) and prices are assumptions, not the paper's process characterisation (which is in its supplement).
