How this model works

Rigid-beam kinematics

Beams are rigid and flexures are revolute joints (Methods 4.2). Compressing each beam row by uy tilts every vertical beam by θ = cos⁻¹((h − uy)/h), giving a lateral offset k = h sin θ (Methods 4.1). The BEU's state EV = ±1 picks the side, so a joint's x-position is the top position plus the running sum of EV·k down its column.

Horizontal crossbars of length w join every other grid point to its neighbour. An encoding is valid only if every crossbar keeps length w in the expressed state (Methods 4.1). In a unit cell (2×2 BEUs between two crossbars) that leaves 6 of 16 combinations: 2 shear cells and 4 cells giving negative, zero or positive Poisson's ratio (Fig. 1A, C).

Shape matching follows Sec. 4.6. The right-hand profile is fitted by a greedy pass from the top and from the bottom, and then a best-first search (capped at 1,000 nodes) over the rows where the two passes disagree. Columns are then filled leftwards: a shear pair is copied, otherwise (+,−) or (−,+) is chosen, whichever lands closer to the target.

Beam length h = 23.4 mm is derived from the paper: each 2-row cell compresses uy,max = 5.5 mm (Methods 4.4), which is 2.75 mm per beam, and θmax = 28° (Sec. 2.4).

What is simplified
  • Purely kinematic, as in the paper: no stresses, no energy barriers. Programming (~0.5 N) and holding (>20 N) forces are reported values from Sec. 1 and 2.1 and are not computed here.
  • Crossbar length w is drawn at an assumed 0.6 h. It does not affect which encodings are valid.
  • Filling the inner columns towards a straight-line blend of the two target sides is this demo's choice. The paper only states that it compares x-values for the two options (SI S7).
  • All beams share one tilt angle at a given compression (the paper's approximation). Real sheets deform unevenly before the final expressed state.
