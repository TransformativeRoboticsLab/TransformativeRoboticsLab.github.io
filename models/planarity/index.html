Flat-Plane Mechanism: planarity from geometry

Illustrative model based on Kowalewski et al., Robots That Generate Planarity Through Geometry (arXiv 2602.06294), Supplementary Text (FPM proof, Eqs. 5–8; design parameters; Eq. 1).

Thirteen links and six joints. The control link FB holds joint B on a sphere through the grounded joint O. The bipyramid inverts B through a sphere centred on O, so the endpoint D can only move on a plane. For measured data from the lab's robotic FPM, see FPM surface probing.

How this model works

Geometry, equations and what is simplified
  • Link lengths from the design parameters (Supplementary Text, “FPM Design Parameters”): A = (Lc − 2H)/2 for OF and FB; B = √(H² + R²) for the six bipyramid links to B and D; C = √((Lc − H)² + R²) for OA, OC, OE; D = √((R + R cos(γ/2))² + (R sin(γ/2))²) for AE, AC.
  • Why D stays flat (proof, Eqs. 5–8): O, B, D are collinear and OB·OD = OE² − ED² = k² is fixed, so D is the inverse of B in a sphere of radius k about O. Because FB = OF, B rides on a sphere through O, and that sphere inverts to a plane. Change FB / OF and the readout shows D moving on a sphere instead (Supplementary “Inversion between two spheres”).
  • Forward kinematics. The demo places A, E, C on the circles where spheres about O and B intersect, uses AE and AC to fix their spacing, and then finds D as the intersection of three spheres. This follows the structure of the paper's forward kinematics (Eqs. 15–44) but is coded directly in vectors. The idle spin of the bipyramid about the OB axis is fixed by a gauge (A is kept in the plane of OB and the x axis).
  • Driving the endpoint. Targets on the plane are mapped back to the control-link angles with ideal inversion (B = k²·D/|D|²), then run through the forward kinematics. The lab's robot uses two motors and revolute joints for this (Eqs. 45–78), which the demo does not model.
  • Sensitivity Sk (Eq. 1) = RMS plane residual ÷ RMS link-length error. As in the paper's Methods: σ is a percentage of Lc on all 13 links, the endpoint visits n = 50 points over a disc of Wd/Lc, and the result is averaged over random instances (50 for the single run, 20 per point on the chart). The paper reports a global minimum Sk,min = 0.072 at H* = 1/4, R* = 1/2, γ* = π/2 (Fig. 2B). This simplified Monte Carlo finds about 0.05–0.06 there. Its point layout, gauge and plane fit differ from the paper's, so compare trends, not exact values.
  • Reported numbers you can compare against (paper text, not computed here): centimeter-scale FPMs with mean link-length RMSE 141.8 µm gave 15.6 µm flatness RMSE (Fig. 2D). The robotic FPM has Lc = 487.5 mm and Wd = 200 mm (Fig. 3).
