Fabrication-directed entanglement: unit cells

Illustrative model based on Revier, Emery, Miske & Lipton, “Fabrication-Directed Entanglement…”, Adv. Eng. Mater. 2025 (arXiv 2505.03064, §3.2, §6.4, Table 1). VTP prints two foam “phases” from one filament: dense coils (E ≈ 3.4 MPa) and sparse coils (E ≈ 0.11 MPa). Pattern them in a unit cell, and periodic homogenization (the paper's FE method) gives the effective stiffness matrix C. That yields anisotropy, Poisson's ratio and normal–shear coupling (chirality).

How this model works

  1. Two phases: element density ρ = 1 is the dense VTP pair (V*, H*) = (0.15, 6.93), and ρ = 0 is the sparse pair (0.40, 14.81) (§6.1). The phases have EHD ≈ 3.4 MPa and ELD ≈ 0.11 MPa (§6.2), and νHD ≈ 0.35 and νLD ≈ 0.45 (§6.3). Both are treated as isotropic and linear elastic in plane stress.
  2. Periodic homogenization (§6.4, after Andreassen & Andreasen 2014): on a 32 × 32 bilinear-quad mesh with periodic boundaries, solve K χA = fA for the three unit strains εA ∈ {ε₁₁, ε₂₂, γ₁₂}. Then CAB = |Y|−1 Σe (u₀A − χA)eT ke (u₀B − χB)e. The solver is preconditioned conjugate gradients in the browser.
  3. Properties (§4): S = C−1, E₁ = 1/S₁₁ and E₂ = 1/S₂₂. νji = −εii/εjj gives ν₁₂ = −S₂₁/S₁₁ and ν₂₁ = −S₁₂/S₂₂. ηkij = γij/εkk gives η₁₁₂ = S₃₁/S₁₁ and η₂₁₂ = S₃₂/S₂₂. A nonzero η needs a pattern without mirror symmetry about the axes, i.e. a chiral or rotated one (§4.2).
  4. Normalized eigenvalues (§4.4, Fig. 4e): λ̂i = λi/λ₃ of C in Mandel form (shear terms scaled by √2 and 2). A homogeneous ν = 0.4 phase sits at (0.43, 0.43), as the paper reports.
  5. Feature thickening: VTP cannot resolve features finer than a coil (≈1.2 mm, §3.1), so thin dense features print thicker. The paper calibrated this with the projection dilation χ (§6.5). Here it is approximated by dilating the dense phase by 0–3 elements. One element is 25 mm / 32 ≈ 0.8 mm for the paper's 150 mm, 6 × 6 arrays.

Simplified: no topology optimization (MMA/eigenstructure target T*), only hand-designed or painted cells. The analysis is 2D, linear and small-strain, so it captures neither the buckling the paper saw above ≈5% strain nor any entanglement or contact mechanics.
