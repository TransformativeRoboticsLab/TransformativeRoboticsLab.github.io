How this model works

TRUNC: torsionally rigid universal coupling

TRUNCs tile an auxetic double-arrowhead cell on a sphere with axial point-group symmetry 2*N (N cells round the equator, here *4 by default). M tilings run between the poles: M = 2 gives the equatorial joint, M = 3 the truss joint. Because the equator stays tied to both poles, twisting is a stiff mode, while bending (antisymmetric cell deformation) and extension (axisymmetric deformation) are soft modes.

Each joint behaves as a constant-velocity (CV) joint. Here the joint bands are placed on the bisecting plane between the two shafts, the classical CV condition, so output phase equals input phase at any bend. The chart compares a conventional Cardan joint (textbook relation tan φout = tan φin/cos β, not from the paper), whose output speed fluctuates.

Measured selectivity is the twist–bend ratio GJ/EI: 11 for the equatorial joint and 52 for the truss joint (spring-steel joints, Fig. 2C–D). The readouts also report: up to 45° bend per joint at 85.7% torque and energy transmission efficiency (fig. S6A); 83.6% of torsional rigidity kept at 20% stretch (Fig. 3B); compression to 13.3% of arm length.

The full TRUNC arm's measured workspace (18,300 motion-capture poses) is in the TRUNC workspace explorer.

BETR: bendable, extendable, torsionally rigid tube

Each cell is a Watt's straight-line linkage: ground pivots 2A apart, two rockers of length B and a coupler of length 2C (Fig. 3B). Its midpoint traces Watt's curve, r² = b² − [a sin θ ± √(c² − a² cos² θ)]² (Eq. 1). Near the centre that curve is almost straight, so a cell extends along one line and resists shear. Validity needs C + B > 1 and |C − B| < 1. The cell tilt is ϕ = arccos((A² + B² − C²)/2AB) (Eq. 2).

As in the paper's Methods, the straight-line range is where the midpoint stays within 1% of the cell width of the line, and ER = (hi + range)/hi. A second value adds supports of 10% of the cell width (Sec. 2.2). Cells are mirrored and tiled (** wallpaper) and wrapped onto a cylinder. Bending is imposed as a constant-curvature arc, so each column's cells extend by the local arc length.

The scaling law (Sec. 2.2, SM8) says axial stiffness ∝ A = π(R² − r²) and torsional stiffness ∝ J = π(R⁴ − r⁴)/2. Defaults are the paper's samples: 25.4 mm OD and 22.23 mm ID, which leaves a 76% lumen.

What is simplified
  • No elastic or FE model runs here. Stiffness numbers are the paper's reported values or the A and J scaling law. Flexure width and length (Fig. 3C) are not modelled.
  • TRUNC arm and cell shapes are schematic, not the laser-cut spring-steel drawings. The rigidity retention between 0% and 20% stretch is a straight line through the two reported points.
  • The BETR cell height definition is approximate, so absolute ER values differ from Fig. 3B (the paper reports an optimum near 1.6× once supports and gaps are added). The trends match: ER grows as C → 0 and B → 1. Beam gaps of 5% are not enforced.
