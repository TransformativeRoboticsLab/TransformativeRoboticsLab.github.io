How this model works

An HSA is a cylinder tiled with shearing-auxetic cells. Twisting one end shears every cell the same way, which drives the internal link angle φ along the auxetic trajectory and lengthens the tube (2022 paper, Sec. I–II, Fig. 2). Its handedness fixes which twist direction extends it, so left and right cylinders cannot swap chirality (2018 paper, Sec. II-B).

Closed HSAs. The readouts and chart use the paper's own empirical fits for 3D-printed FPU50 HSAs with 19 mm outer diameter and 2 mm wall (Table III, θ in degrees): Fb = Aθ² + Bθ, τh = Cτθ, k = Ckθ + k0, L = Clθ + L0. The paper's sign convention gives negative Fb for pushing. Here pushing is shown as positive.

Semi-open and open HSAs (4 rows) have no full fit table. Only the reported coupling constants (0.030 and 0.013 mm per degree), the open HSA's quadratic torque fit τh = −1.219×10⁻⁴θ² + 0.1834θ, and the twist ranges in Table I are used. L0 is taken as the printed length (Table I). Blocked force and stiffness for these are not fitted in the paper, so only the reported numbers are quoted.

Linear actuator. A left and a right HSA are joined at a locked end and counter-rotated by gears at the other end. Their reaction torques cancel, so the pair just extends (2018, Sec. III, Fig. 3). Pair force and stiffness are taken as twice one cylinder's, treating the two as parallel springs.

Bending finger. A constraint line bonded along one side of the pair makes it bend (2018, Sec. V, Fig. 6). Here that side keeps length L(0) while the far side reaches L(θ), giving a constant-curvature arc with bend angle Φ = (L(θ) − L(0)) / d, where d is the outer diameter. This is a kinematic simplification and not a model from the paper.

Simplified: the 3D lattice is a schematic of helical "wide beams" and staggered short links, not the exact cell pattern. The tube radius is held constant, although real HSAs also change diameter. Viscoelastic relaxation is ignored. The 2022 paper reports a time constant of about 3 s, and 45% force loss after 20 min (Sec. III-C).

Reported reference values: 2018 PTFE linear actuator 144–217 mm (Table I). Pneumatic vs HSA gripper: 107.4 J vs 4.92 J to close, and both grasped 72% of 32 objects (Table II). 2022: open HSA up to 124 N pull at −180°. Semi-open 8.9 N push at +90°, closed 11.6 N (Sec. III-B).
