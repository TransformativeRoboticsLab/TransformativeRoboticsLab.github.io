How this model works

A stainless-steel band wraps a shaft covered in a 55 µm polybenzimidazole (PBI) film. With voltage on, electrostatic adhesion pulls the band onto the shaft. That adds a normal force on top of the ordinary capstan normal force, so friction grows exponentially along the wrap (Fig. 2).

Capstan only (Eq. 1): Tload = Thold eµθ.

Electrostatic capstan (Eqs. 2–3): the electrostatic normal force per unit angle is α = β·l·r. Integrating gives Tload = Tholdeµθ + α(eµθ − 1).

JR + Coulomb pressure (Eq. 4): β = (ε0/2)V²[(εgεd/(dεg + gεd))² + (εg/g)²]. The second term is the Johnsen-Rahbek term. Charge migrates through the slightly conductive PBI, so the voltage drops mostly across the micron-scale air gap g rather than the dielectric thickness d.

Planar equivalent (Eq. 5): the same area laid flat gives Tplanar = µβ·l·r·θ. Their ratio is the capstan advantage, Adv ≈ (eµθ − 1)/(µθ) (Eq. 6). It is always above 1.

Torque and band stress: τ = Tload·r. The band stress is Tload/(l·h), and τmax = l·h·r·σmax (Eqs. 7–8).

Parameters (Sec. IV–V): shaft Ø 25.4 mm (r = 12.7 mm), band width l = 10 mm, PBI d = 55 µm, εd = 3.9, εg = 1, µ = 0.20. The fitted gaps are from Fig. 4: 2.3, 2.3, 2.9, 2.9, 3.6 and 4.1 µm for 0.5–3 wraps on the thin band, and 1.9 µm for the polished band at 2.25 wraps. Thold is 0.05 N for the thin band. The thick band used 2 N, modelled as 0.3 N (Sec. V-B).

Reported results quoted: the 25.4 µm band snapped at ≈2.5 N·m (Sec. V-A). The 76.2 µm band held 7.1 N·m at 500 V, which is 31.3 N/cm² at 2.5 mW/cm² (Abstract, Sec. VI).

Simplified: the gap is a free parameter that the paper fitted per wrap angle. Changing the wraps here snaps g to the nearest fitted value, and you can then override it. Power draw, band bending stiffness and peeling are not modelled. The band is drawn as a 10° helix (Sec. IV-A).
