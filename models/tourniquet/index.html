How this model works

All slopes and peak loads are the paper's (Tables 1–6). The rest is a simplified load path of our own: twisting the windlass n half-turns shortens the band by ΔL = nπd, giving band tension T = K·ΔL. The buckle, backing support and clip-side strap carry T (the paper's uniaxial tension tests, Tables 1, 2, 4); the windlass resists a torque of 2T·(d/2), which a hand balances with F = T·d/a at its tip (the single-edge moment test, Table 5) and which loads its centre with about 2T in three-point bending (Table 6). Pressure under the band uses Laplace's law, P = T/(r·w) with r = C/2π. The chart draws each material as a straight line at the reported slope up to the reported peak — an idealisation, not the measured curves (which are in Figs. 5–10).

Not modelled: anisotropy from print orientation (the paper's main explanation for weak printed clips), Velcro slip, and the CAT's one-piece backing/clip (CAT “full” peak 700.2 N vs 391.8 N cut, Table 2).
