Duolingo: translating actions after a dynamics shift

Toy illustration of the idea in the abstract of Vemuri, Wu, Thareja, Chen, Good, Lipton & Gupta, Duolingo: Dynamics Utilization for Online Translation of Actions (ICRA 2025). The full text was not available to us. The paper's algorithm, learned models, tasks and HSA platform are not reproduced here.

The setting, from the abstract: the robot's own actuators drift (fatigue, hysteresis) while the environment's dynamics stay the same. Rather than retraining the controller, a short calibration fits a non-linear action-translation model. Each command the old controller would send is then translated into one that makes the drifted robot do the same thing.

How this toy works

Model, and what it is not
  • Environment (unchanged): a ball rolls on a plate tilted about two axes: ẍ = (5/7)·g·sin(tilt) − c·ẋ, plate ±150 mm. This under-actuated system stands in for the paper's tasks. The paper used simulated tasks and a 4-DoF handed-shearing-auxetic (HSA) platform. Two tilt axes is our simplification.
  • Robot (shifts): a command a ∈ [−1, 1] per axis sets the tilt through the actuator map. Nominally tilt = 6°·a with a first-order lag. After the shift the map becomes 6°·(gain·a + cubic·a³ + offset), passed through a backlash (play) operator for hysteresis.
  • Controller (fixed): a PD law tuned on the nominal robot stands in for the paper's learned policy. It is never retrained.
  • Action translation: the calibration sends evenly spaced commands, sweeping up and then down, and records the steady tilt. It fits a polynomial h(tilt) → command per axis by least squares. The translated command is a′ = h(f0(a)), so the drifted robot reproduces the tilt the controller expected. A static polynomial cannot represent hysteresis, so some error remains when backlash is large.
  • Chart: moving RMS distance from the target for the three runs. These are simulated toy numbers, not results from the paper.
