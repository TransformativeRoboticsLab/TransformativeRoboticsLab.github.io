How this model works

Compatibility matrix → zero modes

Each SLM sits on a lattice bond li and only allows relative motion along ui = R(θi)li (paper's di = I + ξR(θi)l̂i, Eq. S3). Symmetry fixes the other angles (Table S1): 2*22 mirrors (θ₂ = −θ₁), 442 and 632 rotate (θi = θ₁).

With a uniform displacement gradient H = ε + ω and, for the two-node Type II cell, a sublattice shift δ, the bond's relative motion is Δi = Hli + δ. Requiring ni·Δi = 0 (no motion across any SLM) gives a small compatibility matrix; its null space holds every floppy motion. The number of independent strains in that null space is the extremal mode (null-, uni-, bi-, trimode; SI Eq. S5).

For the readouts, each SLM is a spring pair (ks along u, kh across it). Minimizing energy over ω and δ gives the homogenized 3×3 C; its normalized eigenvalues λ̂ = λ/λ₃ place the material in the gamut (Sec. 3.3, Fig. 3). In the rigid limit this reproduces the paper's analytical compliance S(θ) = B K⁻¹ Bᵀ (Eq. S7–S9) exactly.

The square lattices are drawn in the paper's test frame (lattice lines at ±45°, x₁ horizontal) so the axes match Fig. 2.

What is simplified
  • The SLM is idealized as a perfect slider plus a spring; no flexure geometry, FEA or large deformation. Absolute moduli are in units of kh, not MPa.
  • The gamut and curves come from this spring model, not from the paper's FEA, so magnitudes differ (the paper's FEA gives ν ≈ −0.88 for the Type I unimode and +0.97 for the Type II bimode).
  • The dashed "paper fit" curves are the paper's analytical model fitted to its measured data (Table S2). They are not raw measurements.
