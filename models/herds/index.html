How this model works

Hierarchy. HERDS nests two one-degree-of-freedom mechanisms (Fig. 1). The beams of a hexagonal Kresling tower are pop-up extending trusses (PETs). Each PET is a scissor beam whose triangular cross-section lies flat when stowed and pops up when extended. Deployment runs in two phases: first the Kresling untwists with the PETs still stowed, then the PETs extend (Fig. 1b).

PET cell. For phase angle α, the long scissor width is a = (l2 + l3)√(2(1 − cos α)) (Eq. 1). The side-scissor angle and width are β and b (Eqs. 3–4). The cross-section apex angle is θ = cos⁻¹((a² − 2b²)/(−2b²)) (Eq. 7). The pop-up conditions θ = π when stowed (Eq. 17) and θ = π/3 when deployed (Eq. 18) fix the stowed and deployed α. These are solved in closed form here.

Kresling. lAB = 2r sin(π/6) (Eq. 19). The outer members are lBC = √(h² − 2r²cos ϕ + 2r²) (Eq. 20) and the inner members are lAC = √(h² − 2r²cos(2π/6 + ϕ) + 2r²) (Eq. 21). Stowed, n·c₁ = lBC = D/2 (Eq. 24) with D = 2lAB + 2a₁ (Eq. 22), which sets r. Deployed, n·cf = lBC = lAC (Eq. 25), which gives ϕf = −π/6. Total height is m·h, and the expansion ratio is Hf/H0.

Objective. The member aspect ratio (l1 + l2 + l3)/t (Eq. 32) is what the paper's MINLP minimises (Eq. 31). Its Table 4 reports 4–11× better values for HERDS than for a pure PET or a pure Kresling.

Defaults. n = 13, m = 2, H₀ = 0.050 m and member thickness 6 mm are from Table 6 (large prototype: D = 0.578 m, Hf = 2.540 m, reported expansion ratio 50.8). The paper does not list link lengths. l1–l3 were chosen here so that D and Hf come close to Table 6 (0.570 m and 2.546 m).

Simplified. (1) The PET cell length is c = 2·l3·cos(α/2). This comes from the trapezoid-closure constraint, Eq. (11). Eq. (2) as printed did not give the π → π/3 pop-up in our implementation, so it is not used. (2) The PET is drawn as one scissor X per cell on each of its three faces. Joint offsets, member thickness and the thickness bounds (Eqs. 12–16) are not drawn. (3) The two phases are run one after the other, and phase 1 holds the PET length fixed. (4) This is kinematics only: no FEA, loads (Table 5) or deployment dynamics.

Microgravity validation. A follow-up iSpaRo 2025 paper (Fogelson, Thomas et al.) flew a translational scissor, a polar scissor and a PET on a parabolic flight. It recorded their centripetal deployment and fitted the Dojo rigid-body simulator to the motion. Its abstract reports 1.3 cm, 1.2 cm and 4.4 cm RMSE respectively. That paper's dynamics are not reproduced here. This page shows only the PET's kinematics.
