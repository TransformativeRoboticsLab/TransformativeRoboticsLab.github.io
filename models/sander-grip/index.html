Vibration-reducing sander grip

Illustrative model based on Kandukuri, Kashyap & Lipton, Vibration Reduction Using Material Jetted Parts for Sander Grips (SFF 2022). Grips were PolyJet-printed from TangoBlack+ (TBP) with up to 25% trapped non-curing liquid, coated with Loctite Shoe Glue, and tested on a 5″ Sioux sander at a Boeing factory.

Liquid-filled TBP has tan δ between 0.3 and 1 at 1 Hz, rising with frequency as a power law (p. 1952, from MacCurdy et al. 2016).

How this model works

The paper reports measurements, not a vibration model. This page uses the simplest model of its mechanism: the hand is a mass m resting on the pad, a viscoelastic spring with complex stiffness k* = k′(1 + i tan δ), driven by the sander. Transmissibility to the hand is T = |k*| / |k* − mω²|. The pad stiffness is k′ = E′A/h. Following the paper's description, tan δ at 1 Hz rises from 0.3 (solid TBP) to 1 (25% liquid) and grows with frequency as f0.1.

Assumed, not from the paper: E′ of TBP ≈ 1 MPa at 1 Hz, falling linearly to 0.4 MPa at 25% liquid; storage-modulus exponent 0.05; stock rubber grip E′ = 3 MPa, tan δ = 0.1, 3 mm thick; the hand mass; the sander speed. A single-mass hand cannot capture the large worker-to-worker differences the paper saw, which it attributes to grip force.

Coating selection (Tables 1–3): candidates were filtered by peel, compression/torsion and abrasion tests, then by IPA/MEK/acetone exposure and dust/scuff tests; Urecoat and Loctite Shoe Glue survived, and only Shoe Glue bonded to the liquid-filled TBP.
