Baxter's homunculus

Illustrative model based on Lipton, Fay & Rus, Baxter's Homunculus: Virtual Reality Spaces for Teleoperation in Manufacturing (IEEE RA-L 2018; arXiv 1703.01270), Section III, Eqs. 4–9 and Algorithm 1. The operator sits in a virtual control room mapped inside the robot's head and drives each arm by grabbing a virtual gripper orb.

How this model works

The paper decouples user and robot through a virtual space V (Fig. 1D). Robot grippers appear in V by MGG: rV = T̄GG rR + O with T̄GG = λGGR̄GG (Eqs. 4–6); here R̄ is the identity, O places the control room in Baxter's head, and the page inverts this map to turn the orb's position into a robot target. While grabbed, MHG couples the orb to the hand: rV = C̄r rVH, n̂ = C̄n n̂H, θ = C̄θθH (Eqs. 7–9), with C̄ = I for the coupled quantities and 0 otherwise (Algorithm 1). On release a planner checks the target; the orb turns red if it is unreachable and blue if valid (Section IV).

Simplified: Baxter's 7-DOF arm and its motion planner are replaced by a two-link arm with 0.55 m links and a reach-sphere check from each shoulder; dimensions are approximate. No stereo video, latency or headset rendering is simulated.
