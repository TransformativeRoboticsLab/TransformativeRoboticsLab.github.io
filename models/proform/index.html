How this model works

The paper is experimental and gives no forming equations, so this page uses a simplified membrane picture of its process. While formed, the softened sheet carries a membrane tension N = σf·t; air pressure p bends it to a radius R = N/p (Laplace's law), so higher pressure gives a tighter wrap — consistent with the paper's finding that pressures above 58 psi gave sharper details (Section 3.4). Between two parts a gap w is spanned by an arc of radius R that sags δ = R − √(R² − w²/4); if δ is less than the part height the sheet bridges instead of reaching the board. The force pressing a part onto its z-tape while forming is p × footprint (Section 3.1 notes z-tape needs uniform pressure). The sheet wraps the board edge only if the board is elevated (Fig. 2b–c).

Assumed, not from the paper: σf (hot PETG flow stress), package dimensions (typical datasheet sizes) and the membrane idealisation (no friction, no thinning, no cooling shrinkage). Process times (120 s form + 90 s cool at 160 °C) and all numbers in the “Reported” table are from the paper.
