How this model works

Kinergy states proportionalities (Table 2): helical energy Pe ∝ d⁴X²/(D³N) (Eq. 1), spiral energy Pe ∝ b t³θ²/N (Eq. 2), gear ratio G = (R/r)n (Eq. 4), travel/revolutions ∝ X·G or θ·G (Eqs. 5–7), Scotch-yoke stroke ∝ r (Eq. 8) and Geneva step angle ∝ 1/ns (Eq. 10). To give numbers, this page fills in the textbook constants the paper cites (spring theory, its ref. [4]): k = Gsd⁴/(8D³N) with Gs = E/(2(1+ν)), ν = 0.36; spiral kθ = E b t³/(12L) with strip length L = 2πN·(10 mm mean radius); Pe = ½kX² or ½kθθ². A helical spring drives the train through a rack and a 6 mm pinion (Fig. 4f); a spiral spring is coaxial with the first gear (Fig. 4g). The paper limits G to 1/5–5 (Section 6.1); values outside are flagged. Like Kinergy's own preview (Section 3.3.3), motion assumes no friction and Hooke's-law springs. Material moduli and strengths are typical datasheet values, not from the paper; the stress check (helical τ = 8FD/(πd³), spiral σ = 6M/(bt²), shear limit taken as half the tensile strength) is our addition to flag springs that would likely break.

Not modelled: compliant locks (Fig. 3), print tolerances (0.3 mm gear gap, Section 4), and spring fatigue.
