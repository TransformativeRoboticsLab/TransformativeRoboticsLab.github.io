Viscous thread printing: coils to graded foam

Illustrative model based on Lipton & Lipson, Sci. Rep. 2016, and Emery et al., Adv. Sci. 2024 (Eqs. 1–2, Fig. 1, Fig. 7H). A thread falls from height H and coils as the head moves at F. The ratio of head speed to extrusion speed sets coil spacing, and nozzle height sets coil size. Grading these two parameters across a part grades its density and stiffness. Layer height self-stabilization

After Emery et al. Fig. 2F: cubes printed at H* = 3, 6, 9 all settle to HL = ΔZ, because a taller gap gives a taller layer. Here HL uses an illustrative linear surrogate, HL = ΔZ + 0.12 mm·(H* − H*dense). It assumes the dense pair lies on the homogeneous subspace, and each run starts at 0.5×, 1× or 1.5× the dense H*. The nozzle gap after each layer is gap + ΔZ − HL. The paper used a GPR model trained on 300 experiments.

How this model works

  1. Dimensionless controls (Emery Eqs. 1–2; FDE §2.1): V* = F/C and H* = H/Dt, with thread diameter Dt = α·Dn (die swell). The head speed F is V*·C and the nozzle gap H is H*·Dt.
  2. Coil kinematics (geometric "sewing machine" picture, Brun et al. 2015, cited by both papers): the thread is laid at speed C around a coil of diameter Dc. Coil frequency is f = C/(π Dc) and the coil pitch along the path is λc = F/f = π Dc V*. This gives the paper's statement that V* sets linear coil density (§2.1).
  3. Coil size: the paper states Dc ∝ H*. Here Dc = κ·H*·Dt, an assumed proportionality. The default κ = 0.38 gives the ~1.2 mm coil the FDE paper reports for its dense pair (H* = 6.93, §3.1).
  4. Coiling regime (Lipton Fig. 1; Emery Fig. 1C–D): translating coils → alternating loops → meanders → straight line as V* rises. Below H* ≈ 1.5 the thread cannot buckle. The boundaries used here (V* = 0.5, 0.7, 1.0) are approximate. Real boundaries also depend on viscosity.
  5. Density (mass conservation): thread length per unit path is 1/V*, so ρ/ρs = (π Dt2/4)/(V*·ΔL·ΔZ). This holds when the layer height equals ΔZ, the paper's homogeneous subspace (§2.2). Values above 1 are flagged as over-packed: those layers would grow taller than ΔZ.
  6. Stiffness: E = Es(ρ/ρs)B, with B = 3.57 (TPU, Z), 3.62 (TPU, X) and 3.87 (PLA, Z) from Emery Fig. 7H. For silicone, B is this demo's least-squares fit to Lipton Table 1.
  7. Check against the paper: at the FDE parameters (button above; α = 1.15 assumed) the model gives E ≈ 3.8 MPa for the dense pair and ≈ 0.12 MPa for the sparse pair, about 33:1. The FDE paper measured EHD ≈ 3.4 MPa and ELD ≈ 0.11 MPa (§6.2), about 30:1. The flow rate, ≈ 28 g/h, is close to the 30 g/h reported in RoboSoft 2025 §V.
  8. Grading: each map cell holds a blend d ∈ [0, 1]. V* and H* are interpolated linearly between the sparse and dense pairs, like the linear gradient in Emery Fig. 3C.

Simplified: no fluid simulation, no thread–thread contact, no GPR. Coil shapes for loops and meanders are schematic. Es for PLA (3 GPa) and the PLA density are typical datasheet values, not taken from the papers. Es for TPU (12 MPa, 1.19 g/cc) is from the FDE paper §6.1. For silicone, the bulk values (0.608 MPa compression, 1040 kg/m³) are from Lipton Table 1.
