SCCRUB: counter-rotating brush and contact force

Illustrative model based on Kowalewski et al., SCCRUB: Surface Cleaning Compliant Robot Utilizing Bristles (RoboSoft 2026; arXiv 2507.06053), Sec. III (Eqs. 1–7), Sec. IV-B and Sec. V-A (Eqs. 8–11).

A soft, torque-transmitting arm (built on TRUNC) spins a brush against a surface. Bristle friction makes a reaction moment that would bend the soft arm. Two faces spinning in opposite directions cancel most of it.

How this model works

Equations, defaults and simplifications
  • Friction moment (Eqs. 1–4). The bristles are a continuous disc under uniform pressure P, and dynamic friction is tangent to the spin. A ring of radius r and width dr gives dM = 2πμP r² dr, so a full disc gives M = (2/3)πμP r³. With P = Fn/(πr2), that is M = (2/3)μ r Fn.
  • Counter-rotating brush (Eqs. 5–7). The inner disc (radius ri) and the outer annulus (ri to ro) spin opposite ways: Mnet = (2πμP/3)(ro³ − 2ri³). The net moment is zero when ro/ri = 21/3, so ri ≈ 0.79 ro. Here P = Fn/(πro²) over the whole brush face.
  • Defaults from the paper: ro = 35 mm and ri = 25 mm (Sec. IV-A; ro is 11.1% over the ideal), μ ≈ 0.93 (fit to the unidirectional-brush data), ω = 110 RPM. The gearbox has a 16-tooth sun on the inner face and a 50-tooth ring on the outer face with a grounded carrier, so the outer face turns at −16/50 of the input. Backlash δ = 52.4 N·mm (Sec. IV-B), and Fg = 9.6 N with Fn = 3 N in the cleaning tests (Sec. VI).
  • Two counter-rotating slopes. The paper reports M = kF − δ with a predicted k = 9.34 N·mm/N from its expression (2/3)μ(ro − 2ri), against 9.96 N·mm/N measured (Sec. IV-B, Fig. 3B). Evaluating Eq. 7 directly with P = F/(πro²) gives a smaller slope for the same radii. The chart shows both, labelled. The demo plots model lines only, no measured points.
  • Arm statics (Eqs. 8–11). With slow translation the arm is quasi-static: Ft + Fn − Fg = 0, so the tendon tension is Ft = Fg − Fn. The paper feeds (x, Ft) to a learned network (Eq. 12) to get the nine tendon lengths. The network and its 10,000-pose dataset are not reproduced here. The demo only checks whether Ft stays inside the trained load range (6.2 to 15.3 N), which caps Fn at Fg − 6.2 N (Sec. V-C).
  • The arm drawing is a schematic, not a simulation of its deflection.
