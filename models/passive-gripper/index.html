Computational design of passive grippers

Illustrative model based on Kodnongbua, Good, Lou, Lipton & Schulz, Computational Design of Passive Grippers (ACM TOG 41(4), 2022; arXiv 2306.03174), Secs. 4–5 and 7.2. A 2D re-implementation that keeps the paper's pipeline but simplifies the robot and the final shape step.

A passive gripper has no motors. The robot arm's motion does the grasping. Given an object, the algorithm picks three contact points that hold it against gravity. It then designs a gripper skeleton together with an insert trajectory that slides the gripper onto those points without hitting the object.

How this model works

What follows the paper and what is simplified
  • GC sampling (Sec. 4, 7.2): 1,000 random surface points, excluding floor contacts, are combined into 3,000 random three-point grasp configurations (GCs). Each point is offset 0.5 mm outward, as the paper offsets by half the printer resolution.
  • Stability (Sec. 4.1): each contact has a Coulomb friction cone with μi = max(0, ni·g)·μ, so top-facing contacts get no friction. A GC is kept if non-negative cone wrenches can balance gravity (partial force closure). In 2D a wrench is (fx, fz, τ/size), and the cone test is exact.
  • Reachability (Sec. 4.2): keep a GC only if some instantaneous rigid motion (v, ω, c) breaks every contact within θmax = 80°. The search descends the barrier loss Σ[cos θmax − vi·ni]+ + [‖vi‖ − 1]+.
  • Ranking (Sec. 4.3): rank by partial minimum wrench (here, the distance from the gravity wrench to the cone boundary) and by estimated finger length (the longest shortest collision-free path from the flange). Non-dominated sorting forms Pareto fronts, and each front is sorted by finger length.
  • Co-optimisation (Sec. 5): the skeleton has three fingers with m = 4 joints, and the trajectory has n = 4 keyframes. The objective is Eg + Et + λ1Er + λ2L. Path collision is the inside distance plus the wrap-around distance, here the shorter way round the 2D outline, standing in for the mesh geodesic. The search is controlled random search with local mutation, finger joints may move ±10 mm (as in the paper), and λ1 = 1000. The demo stops at the first collision-free design.
  • Simplified: 2D sections instead of meshes. A planar flange (x, z, rotation) stands in for the UR5's six joints. The population is 160 instead of 10,000, keyframes may move ±60 mm and ±60°, and the floor clearance is h = 20 mm to suit the smaller 2D objects. The time and skeleton sampling are coarser. The final step, topology optimisation of the gripper in the collision-free volume (Sec. 6), is replaced by thickening the skeleton.
  • Reported results (paper, not computed here): solutions were found for all 23 experiments, and 21 of the printed grippers picked up their object in the real tests (Sec. 7, Table 2).
