Readout

Load the layer can hold against twisting versus vertical slip x of the object (Eq. 5/6) for every layer the papers report. The flat dashed level is the friction limit μFn (Eq. 1). The grasp fails at whichever limit is lower.

How this model works

A soft finger grasping an object antipodally can fail three ways (2026 paper, Sec. III, Fig. 2): it slips because normal force is too low, it twists out of plane, or the soft pad shears.

Slip: Fstatic = μFn (Eq. 1), with μ = 1.27 for Ecoflex on painted Vero (Sec. VI-A).
Twist: the payload's weight Fg at lever arm r makes a moment M = Fgr (Eq. 2), resisted by the layer's torsional stiffness κ: M = κθ (Eq. 3). With slip x = rθ (Eq. 4), Fg = (κ / r²)·x (Eq. 5).
HSA gripper: the HSA's own reaction torque adds cτφ / rh (Eq. 6–7) when its chirality is chosen to resist slip.

κ values are the papers' FEA results for PA-6 layers 100–102 mm long, computed with κ = M/θ (Eq. 7 / Eq. 9). Flat layers come from Table I (2026, 102 × 25 mm). The TR-SLL values come from the text: 518.7 and 964.76 N·mm/rad for 2 and 5 triangles (2026), and 366.6 and 265.5 N·mm/rad for 2 and 30 triangles (Pneu-net paper). The two papers used slightly different geometries, so values for the same triangle count differ. Only the reported designs are offered, and no curve is interpolated between them.

Assumptions: rt defaults to 20 mm, which is not reported. rh = 17.1 mm is back-computed from the paper's predicted HSA contribution of 1.47 N with cτ = 0.21 N·mm/° at 120° (Sec. VI-A). The model is linear, so large flat-layer twists (beyond about 30°) are outside its validity. The 3D view twists the layer uniformly along its length about its base.

Reported results: 5.8 N peak pinch and 14.6 N planar caging hold, with grasp stiffness 1.64 and 3.06 N/mm over the first 3 mm (2026, Sec. VI-A). 37/43 YCB objects succeeded, and 5 kg was lifted through the skeleton. The Pneu-net TRL reduced out-of-plane bending by up to 97.7% (2023 abstract).
